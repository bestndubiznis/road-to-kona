Why Kona

Kona has always carried more meaning for me than just being another race. Triathlon has been part of my family for generations, and this is a chance to take on the race they both cared about most while also raising money for something worthwhile.

Family connection

A race that has been in my family for a long time.

My grandfather was a triathlete and had always wanted to race Kona, but never got the chance before he passed away. My dad also raced triathlons and made it to Kona as a spectator, but never had the opportunity to compete there himself. Racing it now feels like a pretty meaningful continuation of that story.

FAQ

A little more context on me, Kona, Team IMF, and the Foundation.

I'm Walker Wells. I live in San Francisco and spend most of my time building a company. Outside of work, endurance sports have become a major part of my life, and this site is my public log of the build to Kona.

There is a family connection. My grandfather was into triathlon and had always wanted to race Kona, but passed away before he had the chance. My dad also raced triathlons and attended Kona as a spectator, but never raced it himself. Getting to compete there feels meaningful for that reason beyond the race itself.

The IRONMAN World Championship in Kona is the sport's iconic full-distance championship in Hawaiʻi: a 2.4-mile swim, 112-mile bike, and 26.2-mile marathon.

I was selected for one of a limited number of Team IMF World Championship entries. My entry is secured through the program, and I've committed to raise $50,000 for the IRONMAN Foundation by September 2027.

The money supports the IRONMAN Foundation's charitable work, including youth swim safety, bikes and helmets for kids, IRONKIDS scholarships, food banks and community services, schools and youth programs, and local nonprofit grants. The impact section above highlights the programs that stood out most to me.
